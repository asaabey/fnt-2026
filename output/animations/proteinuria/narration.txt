OPTIONAL VOICE-OVER OUTLINE

Normal handling
Most albumin stays in the blood. Small proteins pass into the filtrate and
are taken up by proximal tubular cells, leaving very little protein in urine.

Glomerular
If the glomerular barrier becomes less selective, more albumin enters the
filtrate. Some is still taken up, but more reaches the urine. Diabetic kidney
disease with predominantly urinary albumin is the example here.

Tubular
Small proteins are normally filtered. When proximal tubular uptake is
impaired, more of these proteins escape into urine. The main problem is
reduced reabsorption.

Overflow
A plasma-cell disorder may produce excessive free light chains. Their
filtered load can exceed tubular uptake capacity. Uptake continues, but
excess light chains reach the urine. This is overflow proteinuria.

Comparison
Barrier leakage, impaired uptake and excessive filtered load are three
different predominant mechanisms. More than one can coexist in a patient.
