At low plasma osmolality, vasopressin is suppressed and urine is dilute.

Now follow the cursor as water is lost. The sodium amount stays the same,
but there is less water relative to sodium, so plasma osmolality rises.

Vasopressin rises. The kidneys conserve more water, producing a smaller
volume of more concentrated urine.

Thirst provides another response: replacing water by drinking.

Notice the orange plateau. Once the kidney reaches its concentrating limit,
vasopressin can continue to rise without much further increase in urine
concentration. Conserving water has a ceiling.

After drinking water is absorbed, water increases relative to sodium and
plasma osmolality falls. Follow the cursor back along the same curves.
The drive to retain and drink water decreases: negative feedback.

Vasopressin conserves water. Thirst replaces water.

Teaching note: these are schematic curves. Thirst and vasopressin thresholds
vary and can overlap. The horizontal axis is osmolality, not time.
