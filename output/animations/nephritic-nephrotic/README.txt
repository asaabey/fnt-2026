TWO PATTERNS OF GLOMERULAR INJURY

An original layered capillary illustration and captioned Manim animation,
informed by the user's supplied diagram. It follows two separate injury
pathways from a healthy filtration barrier to the clinical patterns in Q5.

Design
The reference informs the three-layer anatomy, red-cell/protein leakage
and distinction between healthy, nephritic and nephrotic patterns. The new
illustration is drawn with editable Manim vector objects in the established
dark-background renal teaching style. Coral denotes red cells, teal denotes
albumin, gold denotes podocytes, pink denotes endothelium and blue denotes
the glomerular basement membrane. Small pale particles suggest filtrate.
No course, week, learning-objective or answer-choice labels appear.

Sequence
1. Healthy: identify fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane and podocyte
   foot processes/slit diaphragms. Blood cells and most albumin stay inside.
2. Inflammatory injury: recruit inflammatory cells and mediators; show
   swelling, reduced filtration and a local barrier defect.
3. Nephritic leakage: allow red cells and some protein into the filtrate;
   associate this with haematuria, reduced GFR, hypertension and 1 g/day protein.
4. Cast formation: show red cells becoming embedded in a tubular matrix in
   a separate downstream tubule inset.
5. Healthy reset: explicitly return to the healthy reference state before
   following a second injury pathway.
6. Podocyte injury: flatten/efface foot processes and mark slit-barrier
   dysfunction, increasing albumin passage without red-cell leakage in
   this illustrative nephrotic case.
7. Nephrotic features: connect heavy proteinuria of 5.2 g/day and plasma
   albumin of 20 g/L with oedema and hyperlipidaemia.
8. Comparison: display the two illustrated states with the question's
   dominant findings and acknowledge possible overlap.

Physiological interpretation and limits
The circle is one glomerular capillary cross-section. The multiple loops,
mesangium and full Bowman's capsule of an entire glomerulus are omitted.
Layer thickness, cell size, fenestrations and slit spacing are schematic.
Some fenestrations are indicated symbolically rather than as a 3D pore map.
The inflammatory route is illustrative. It does not assign a specific
immune-complex, anti-GBM or pauci-immune diagnosis to the question's patient.
The exaggerated local defect visualises loss of barrier integrity; a large
visible basement-membrane tear is not required in every nephritic disease.
Inflammatory narrowing and lower pale-particle flux signify reduced
filtration, without predicting GFR numerically.
Cast formation is shown in a downstream tubule. The shaded matrix represents
uromodulin-containing material, not clotting within a glomerular capillary.
The podocyte pathway illustrates a common nephrotic mechanism. Foot-process
effacement is a structural correlate, not a complete explanation of every
proteinuric disease. Slit-diaphragm dysfunction and interactions between
barrier layers are implicit. A universal loss-of-GBM-charge mechanism or
conversion of the membrane to positive charge is not asserted.
Most albumin is retained by the healthy barrier, rather than all albumin
being absolutely excluded. Subsequent proximal tubular protein processing
is outside this capillary-level scene; filtered protein and final urinary
protein excretion are not a one-to-one particle count.
Low oncotic pressure and renal sodium retention both contribute to oedema.
The nephrotic panel does not imply that all oedema is explained by albumin
loss alone, or that GFR and blood pressure are normal in every nephrotic
patient. Nephritic disease can also have oedema and nephrotic-range protein
loss. The two states depict the dominant patterns in the supplied cases.
The branches are separated by a healthy reset: this is not a claim that
nephritic disease routinely progresses into nephrotic disease.
Particle motion, leakage, opacity, cell deformation and elapsed video time
are qualitative. The timing is an explanatory sequence rather than a
clinical disease timeline or a calibrated physiological simulation.
The reference image is retained as user-provided context. The finished
illustration and animation are original layered Manim drawings.

Reproduce
With Python 3.13 and the pinned Manim dependency installed:
manim -r 1920,1080 --fps 30 --media_dir /tmp/glomerular-render nephritic_nephrotic.py GlomerularInjury -o nephritic-nephrotic.mp4

For quick illustration review, set GLOMERULAR_REVIEW=healthy, nephritic or
nephrotic and use -s. The full render also produces the closing comparison
and writes chapter-times.json beside the source.
