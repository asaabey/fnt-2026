OPTIONAL VOICE-OVER OUTLINE

Healthy barrier
This is a cross-section through a glomerular capillary. The filtration
barrier combines fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane and podocyte
foot processes. Blood cells and most albumin stay inside the capillary.

Inflammatory injury
In an inflammatory pattern, cells and mediators injure the barrier.
Filtration falls, and red cells and some protein enter the filtrate.
The resulting nephritic presentation can include haematuria, hypertension
and reduced kidney function.

Red-cell casts
After entering the tubule, red cells can become trapped in a protein matrix.
This produces a red-cell cast downstream of the injured glomerulus.

Separate pathway
Return to the healthy reference to follow a second pattern of injury.

Podocyte injury
Podocyte foot processes flatten and the slit barrier becomes dysfunctional.
More albumin crosses into the filtrate. Heavy urinary protein loss lowers
plasma albumin. Low oncotic pressure and renal sodium retention contribute
to oedema; hyperlipidaemia is another feature of the nephrotic syndrome.

Comparison
These are dominant patterns rather than completely separate categories.
Nephritic and nephrotic features can overlap in the same disease.
