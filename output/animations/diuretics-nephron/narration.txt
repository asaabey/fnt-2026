Overview
Use the same nephron to locate each drug's action. The anatomical names are
primary; the small site numbers provide the traditional I–IV cross-reference.
The inset has tubular fluid on the left, a tubular cell centrally and
interstitium on the right.

Mannitol
Mannitol is filtered and poorly reabsorbed. It increases tubular-fluid
osmolality and reduces water reabsorption in water-permeable segments.
Its action is osmotic rather than receptor or channel blockade.

Acetazolamide
In the proximal tubule, carbonic anhydrase inhibition reduces bicarbonate
reclamation and associated sodium reabsorption. More bicarbonate, sodium
and water are excreted.

SGLT2 inhibitors
Blocking proximal sodium–glucose cotransport increases glucose delivery to
urine. Glucose contributes to osmotic diuresis. Sodium handling also changes,
although the natriuretic response varies over time and with clinical setting.

Loop diuretics
Furosemide and related drugs block NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb. They
increase sodium excretion and weaken the medullary osmotic gradient. The
ascending limb does not become water-permeable. A weaker gradient reduces
the maximum urine concentration the kidney can achieve.

Thiazides
Thiazides and thiazide-like drugs inhibit NCC in the distal convoluted tubule.
Less sodium and chloride are reabsorbed there. NCC is a different target
from NKCC2.

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists
Spironolactone and eplerenone act at the intracellular mineralocorticoid
receptor, reducing aldosterone-dependent transport. Reduced electrogenic
sodium reabsorption reduces a driving force for potassium secretion.

ENaC blockers
Amiloride and triamterene directly block apical epithelial sodium channels.
The direct target differs from spironolactone, but both groups spare potassium.
Potassium handling also depends on delivery, flow and other physiological factors.

Tolvaptan
Tolvaptan blocks basolateral vasopressin V2 receptors. Less signalling means
less apical aquaporin-2 insertion, with retrieval into intracellular vesicles.
The resulting predominantly water-excreting effect is aquaresis.

Summary
Match the segment to the molecular target. Natriuresis means increased sodium
excretion; aquaresis means increased water excretion relatively independently
of electrolyte excretion. The drugs share a nephron, but not one mechanism.
