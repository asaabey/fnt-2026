LOOP DIURETICS AND URINE CONCENTRATION

This animation explains why NKCC2 blockade reduces maximum urine-concentrating
capacity despite high circulating vasopressin. It is a companion to the urine-
concentration question in the teaching set, with no course-specific labels.

Visual design
Matches the renal-pressure animation: dark #10151F background, Avenir Next,
white main text, muted anatomical labels, teal emphasis and sparse captions.
In this scene, teal particles represent water and gold particles show net
salt transport. The drug label sits beside the ascending limb; the high-
vasopressin label sits beside the collecting duct. There are no week,
objective, exam-answer or branding labels.

Sequence
1. Follow descending and ascending tubular flow beside a collecting duct.
2. Build the medullary gradient while NKCC2-mediated salt reabsorption is active.
3. Show water recovery with high vasopressin and a strong gradient.
4. Block NKCC2 with a loop diuretic and reduce medullary salt accumulation.
5. Weaken the gradient and water recovery while water pathways stay open.
6. Conclude that high permeability cannot fully replace the osmotic driving force.

Physiological interpretation and limits
NKCC2 is an apical cotransporter in the thick ascending limb, not a direct
ATP-driven pump. The salt arrows illustrate net NaCl reabsorption; K+
recycling, basolateral transport and individual epithelial cells are omitted.
Water does not follow NaCl across the water-impermeable ascending limb.

Vasopressin-dependent collecting-duct permeability is held high throughout.
The diuretic does not turn off vasopressin or close the displayed water paths.
The membrane markers denote water pathways, not an anatomically detailed
map of all apical and basolateral transporters.

The medullary gradient is reduced, not abolished. Urea recycling, vasa recta
countercurrent exchange, thin-limb heterogeneity and other determinants are
not separately animated. The simplified descending-limb water movement is
not intended to describe every deep thin-limb segment. Deeper medullary
physiology is more complex than a uniform countercurrent-multiplier model.

The diagrams, colour gradient, particle counts and bars are qualitative and
not calibrated measurements. Particle streams show direction, rather than
a mass-conserving molecular simulation. The outcome is reduced maximum
concentrating capacity; this does not assert that every patient must produce
absolutely hypotonic urine after a loop diuretic.

Re-render
Install Python, uv, Cairo, Pango and FFmpeg as appropriate for your platform.
From this directory:
  uv venv .venv
  uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python -r requirements.txt
  .venv/bin/manim -r 1920,1080 --fps 30 urine_concentration.py UrineConcentration

For a static review of the strong-gradient or post-blockade view:
  URINE_REVIEW=before .venv/bin/manim -ql -s urine_concentration.py UrineConcentration
  URINE_REVIEW=after .venv/bin/manim -ql -s urine_concentration.py UrineConcentration
