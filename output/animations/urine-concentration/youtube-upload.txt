TITLE
Why Loop Diuretics Reduce Urine Concentration | NKCC2 and ADH

DESCRIPTION
Why can a loop diuretic reduce urine-concentrating capacity even when vasopressin is high? Follow salt transport in the thick ascending limb, formation of the medullary osmotic gradient and water movement out of the collecting duct.

The animation shows how NKCC2 inhibition weakens the gradient while vasopressin-dependent water permeability remains high. The key idea: water needs both a permeable pathway and an osmotic driving force.

A silent, captioned animation for medical students and renal physiology revision. The model illustrates reduced maximum concentrating capacity; the medullary gradient is weakened rather than abolished.
