WATER LOADING AND DILUTE URINE

A captioned, silent animation of the renal response to a water load in a
healthy adult. The design follows the urine-concentration animation, with
a magnified collecting duct showing regulated AQP2 movement.

Sequence
1. Establish baseline plasma dilution and collecting-duct water recovery.
2. Add water while keeping the sodium-symbol count fixed.
3. Show slightly lower plasma osmolality and reduced vasopressin release.
4. Move most displayed AQP2 markers from the apical membrane into intracellular
   vesicles; reduce the collecting duct's water permeability.
5. Reduce water recovery, retain more water in tubular fluid and increase
   the volume of dilute urine.
6. Show the plasma compartment moving towards baseline as excess water is
   excreted, limiting dilution of plasma sodium.

Visual design
Dark #10151F background, Avenir Next, white captions, muted anatomy and
teal/gold emphasis match the preceding renal animations. Teal dots represent
water; gold dots in the plasma compartment represent sodium. Labels sit
beside the relevant structures. No course, week, learning-objective or
exam-answer labels appear.

Physiological interpretation and limits
The plasma reservoir illustrates dilution with added water. Its fixed sodium
symbols distinguish concentration from sodium amount during the illustrated
water-handling response. Actual body-water distribution spans several
compartments; the reservoir is a qualitative cue, not a compartment model.
Counterions and other contributors to plasma osmolality are implicit.
Osmotic sensing and hypothalamic/posterior-pituitary physiology are summarised
by the causal arrow and captions; those structures are not separately drawn.
AQP2 markers move from the luminal/apical membrane into intracellular vesicles.
The cell rectangles are principal cells. Basolateral water-path markers remain
in place; the animation focuses on short-term apical AQP2 regulation.
Residual apical AQP2 and water movement remain visible, representing reduced
rather than literally zero water permeability.
The medullary shading stays fixed to isolate the permeability mechanism.
Vasopressin also affects urea and other renal transport processes, which are
outside the depicted mechanism. Diluting-segment solute transport and distal
fluid delivery are assumed intact in this healthy-adult example.
The final plasma state moves towards baseline while the diuretic response is
still depicted. It illustrates negative-feedback direction rather than a
complete steady-state endpoint or persistent diuresis after full correction.
Bar lengths, water levels, channel numbers, particle counts and motion speeds
are qualitative; no osmolality, sodium concentration, urine volume, response
time or molecular flux can be read from them. The particle system is not a
mass-conserving fluid simulation.

Reproduce
With Python 3.13 and the pinned Manim dependency installed:
manim -r 1920,1080 --fps 30 --media_dir /tmp/water-load-render water_load.py WaterLoad -o water-load.mp4

For fast frame review, set WATER_LOAD_REVIEW=before or =after and use -s.
The full render writes chapter-times.json beside the scene source.
