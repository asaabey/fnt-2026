EDELMAN-ROSE GAMBLEGRAM — MINIMAL THREE-PANEL EDIT

Current version: three persistent clinical groups with the simplified equation, 54 seconds.
The prior five-situation captioned prototype is preserved in versions/v1/.
The minimal three-panel version without the equation is in versions/v2/.

Arrangement
Left: SIADH — illustrative water-predominant retention.
Centre: DI — water loss that is not replaced.
Right: nephrotic syndrome, CCF and liver disease — one shared oedematous
sodium/water-balance pattern.

All three panels are present from the start. They begin from the same
reference once. SIADH changes first, then DI; the right panel first retains
sodium and water in balance, then gains more water relative to cations.
The changed panels remain visible throughout. There are no normal-reset cycles.

On-screen content
Exact title: Edelman-Rose Gamblegram.
Simplified equation below the title: Plasma [Na+] ≈ (Na_e + K_e) / TBW.
Clinical group names; one necessary DI qualifier (unreplaced water loss).
Plasma sodium numbers with one shared unit label.
One compact colour key: gold Na+, grey K+, teal H2O.

Removed: narrative captions, mechanistic paragraphs, per-bar
labels, baseline text, relative-water callout labels, repeated explanations,
separate generic sodium-loss and water-gain scenarios, and the separate
water-replacement sequence. No narration or audio is included.

Model
The internal illustrative Rose calculation remains:
plasma sodium ≈ (exchangeable Na + exchangeable K) / total body water.
Potassium is fixed; sodium and water change. The approximate equation is also displayed below the title; the subscript e
identifies exchangeable sodium and potassium.
The reference inputs are Na = 2800 mmol, K = 2800 mmol and TBW = 40 L.
Combined-cation and water bars are each normalised to their own reference
total, giving matched heights at the model's reference sodium of 140 mmol/L.
Equal heights do not equate millimoles to litres. Grey and gold segments
share the cation-total scale. The dashed line is a fixed visual reference.
Hatching compares water with the reference-concentration-matched cation
amount: teal excess, coral deficit. It does not denote a physical compartment.
Values and timings are illustrative, not patient forecasts or correction rates.

Clinical interpretation
SIADH: the unchanged sodium amount is an idealised water-predominant example.
Actual SIADH can include compensatory natriuresis and variable sodium stores.
DI: the displayed 160 mmol/L requires unreplaced water loss. Intact thirst
and adequate drinking can maintain normal sodium despite persistent DI.
The small qualifier stays on screen so hypernatraemia is not presented as
an inevitable diagnostic feature.
Oedematous group: the common diagram represents sodium and water retention,
with or without disproportionate water gain. It does not imply identical
pathogenesis. CCF commonly involves reduced cardiac output; oedematous
advanced liver disease/cirrhosis involves altered vascular filling; nephrotic
oedema can involve underfill, primary renal sodium retention, or both.
The right panel's initial 140 mmol/L phase preserves the lesson that oedema
and sodium retention do not inevitably imply a low plasma sodium.
The later 128 mmol/L phase illustrates hypervolaemic hypotonic hyponatraemia.
These diagrams alone cannot establish a diagnosis or quantify intravascular
versus interstitial fluid distribution. The scope is body-amount geometry.
