OPTIONAL VOICE-OVER OUTLINE

Reference
Plasma sodium depends on exchangeable sodium plus potassium relative to
body water. Grey potassium stays fixed here. The bars show body amounts,
each scaled to its own baseline.

Sodium and water retention
More sodium does not necessarily mean a higher concentration. If total
cations and water rise in proportion, sodium stays normal despite fluid
accumulation and possible oedema.

Water gain
Water gain without added cations lowers the concentration. The hatched
region shows water excess relative to the reference concentration.

Sodium depletion
Both sodium and water can fall while sodium concentration also falls.
Relative water excess does not require an absolute increase in body water.

Oedematous hyponatraemia
Sodium stores may be increased, but a still larger relative water gain
lowers sodium concentration. In heart failure, congestion can coexist with
reduced effective arterial filling and continued vasopressin release.

Diabetes insipidus
DI produces large volumes of dilute urine through vasopressin deficiency
or renal resistance. Sodium rises if water loss exceeds replacement.
If drinking keeps pace, sodium can remain normal even while polyuria persists.

Comparison
Similar sodium concentrations can conceal very different sodium stores.
Assess concentration and volume status separately.
