Q2 — Renal sodium conservation after gastrointestinal bleeding

A 67-year-old man presents with two days of melaena and dizziness on standing. His blood pressure is 92/58 mmHg, pulse is 112/min and urine output has fallen. Urine sodium is 8 mmol/L. He is not taking diuretics. Which renal response contributes to sodium conservation in this situation?

A. ↑ NaCl transport at the macula densa → ↑ renin release.
B. ↑ afferent arteriolar stretch → ↑ renin release.
C. ↓ renal sympathetic beta-1 stimulation → ↑ renin release.
D. ↓ NaCl transport at the macula densa → ↑ renin release from juxtaglomerular granular cells.

Q3 — Hypertension with hypokalaemia

A 46-year-old woman is assessed for persistent hypertension and muscle weakness. Her blood pressure is 168/104 mmHg and plasma potassium is 2.8 mmol/L. She is not taking diuretics and has no vomiting or diarrhoea. Investigation shows elevated plasma aldosterone with suppressed renin. Which principal-cell change best links her hypertension and hypokalaemia?

A. ↑ apical ENaC activity + ↑ basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase activity.
B. ↑ apical aquaporin-2 insertion without ↑ sodium transport.
C. Inhibition of basolateral Na+/K+-ATPase, ↓ cellular potassium uptake.
D. ↓ apical ENaC activity, making the tubular lumen less negative.

Q7 — Oedema with hyponatraemia

A 38-year-old woman with nephrotic syndrome presents with worsening leg swelling and abdominal distension. She has gained 6 kg over two weeks. Plasma sodium is 126 mmol/L, measured plasma osmolality is 264 mOsm/kg and blood glucose is normal. She has not taken diuretics and has had no vomiting or diarrhoea. Which pattern of total-body sodium and water best accounts for these findings?

A. ↓ total-body sodium + ↓ water; proportionately greater ↓ in sodium.
B. ↑ total-body sodium + ↑ water; proportionately greater ↑ in water.
C. ↑ total-body sodium + ↑ water; proportionately greater ↑ in sodium.
D. ↑ total-body sodium + ↑ water; sodium-to-water relationship preserved.

Q12 — IgA nephropathy and infection-associated haematuria

B. Strong linear IgG staining along the glomerular basement membrane
C. A full-house pattern with multiple immunoglobulins and complement components

Q13 — Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis

A 10-year-old develops cola-coloured urine, periorbital oedema and hypertension about two weeks after documented streptococcal pharyngitis. Urine contains red-cell casts and serum C3 is low. Which process best explains the glomerular injury?

B. Autoantibody binding to type IV collagen with linear IgG deposition
C. ANCA-mediated neutrophil injury producing a predominantly pauci-immune lesion
D. Immune-mediated glomerular injury with complement activation and typically granular immune deposits

Q14 — Lupus nephritis and the role of kidney biopsy

A patient with established systemic lupus erythematosus develops proteinuria of 1.8 g/day, red-cell casts and a creatinine rise from 80 to 150 micromol/L over three weeks. C3 and C4 are low. Which approach best establishes the renal lesion and helps guide treatment?

A. Use the anti-double-stranded DNA titre alone to assign the histological class of lupus nephritis.
B. Defer renal investigation until protein excretion exceeds the nephrotic-range threshold.
C. Arrange prompt nephrology assessment and kidney biopsy, if feasible, to assess class, activity and chronicity.
D. Use renal ultrasonography to determine the glomerular immune-deposit pattern and histological class.
