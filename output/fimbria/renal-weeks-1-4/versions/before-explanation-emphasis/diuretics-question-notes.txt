DIURETIC QUESTIONS — TEACHER NOTES
Question labels match the topic-sorted main lesson: Q4 and Q5.

Q4
Consider three diuretics: mannitol, furosemide and hydrochlorothiazide. Which option correctly matches each drug to its main renal mechanism of action?

A. Mannitol: retains water osmotically in tubular fluid; furosemide: blocks NCC in the distal convoluted tubule; hydrochlorothiazide: blocks NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb.
B. Mannitol: retains water osmotically in tubular fluid; furosemide: blocks NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb; hydrochlorothiazide: blocks NCC in the distal convoluted tubule.
C. Mannitol: blocks ENaC in the collecting system; furosemide: blocks NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb; hydrochlorothiazide: blocks NCC in the distal convoluted tubule.
D. Mannitol: retains water osmotically in tubular fluid; furosemide: blocks V2 receptors in the collecting duct; hydrochlorothiazide: blocks NCC in the distal convoluted tubule.

Correct answer: B. Mannitol is a filtered, poorly reabsorbed osmole → ↓ tubular water reabsorption, especially in water-permeable segments. Furosemide inhibits apical NKCC2 (Na+/K+/2Cl− cotransporter) in the water-impermeable thick ascending limb. Hydrochlorothiazide inhibits apical NCC (Na+/Cl− cotransporter) in the distal convoluted tubule.

A swaps the loop and thiazide targets. C assigns the ENaC-blocking mechanism of amiloride to mannitol. D assigns the V2-antagonist mechanism of tolvaptan to furosemide.

NKCC2 blockade → weaker medullary osmotic gradient → ↓ maximum urine-concentrating capacity, even with vasopressin present. Osmotic diuresis and natriuresis are distinct from selective aquaresis. Sources: D1–D2 in diuretics-question-notes.txt.

Q5
Spironolactone and amiloride are potassium-sparing diuretics; tolvaptan promotes aquaresis. Which option correctly identifies their direct renal targets?

A. Spironolactone: ENaC; amiloride: mineralocorticoid receptor; tolvaptan: vasopressin V2 receptor.
B. Spironolactone: mineralocorticoid receptor; amiloride: ENaC; tolvaptan: aquaporin-2 water channel.
C. Spironolactone: mineralocorticoid receptor; amiloride: ENaC; tolvaptan: vasopressin V2 receptor.
D. Spironolactone: mineralocorticoid receptor; amiloride: Na+/K+-ATPase; tolvaptan: vasopressin V2 receptor.

Correct answer: C. Spironolactone blocks the intracellular mineralocorticoid receptor → ↓ aldosterone-driven sodium transport. Amiloride directly blocks apical epithelial sodium channels (ENaC). Both cause ↓ electrogenic sodium reabsorption in the aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron → ↓ driving force for potassium secretion.

Tolvaptan blocks basolateral V2 receptors in collecting-duct principal cells → ↓ signalling for apical aquaporin-2 (AQP2) insertion. Result: ↑ water excretion relatively independently of electrolyte excretion — aquaresis.

A swaps spironolactone and amiloride targets. B mistakes the downstream water channel for tolvaptan’s direct receptor target. D mistakes the basolateral sodium pump for amiloride’s apical channel target. The pump maintains gradients but is not directly inhibited by amiloride. Sources: D3–D5 in diuretics-question-notes.txt.

EXPLANATION SLIDES

Mannitol, loop diuretics and thiazides
Correct answer: B.

Mannitol: a filtered osmole remains in tubular fluid and opposes water reabsorption. It does not need to block a receptor or channel.

Furosemide: blocks apical NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb.

Hydrochlorothiazide: blocks apical NCC in the distal convoluted tubule.

Option A swaps NKCC2 and NCC. Learning point: identify the nephron segment as well as the transporter.

Diuretic mechanisms and molecular targets
Option C is wrong: mannitol acts osmotically; amiloride is the ENaC blocker.

Option D is wrong: furosemide blocks NKCC2; tolvaptan is the V2 antagonist.

Mannitol, loops and thiazides can cause ↑ water excretion, but they do not share one molecular target.

Learning point: ↑ urine volume does not make every drug a selective aquaretic.

Medullary osmotic gradient and collecting-duct water permeability
The loop system helps establish a medullary osmotic gradient; urea recycling contributes, especially in the inner medulla.

Vasa recta countercurrent exchange helps preserve the gradient.

Vasopressin acts at V2 receptors, promoting apical aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting-duct principal cells. Water then moves down an osmotic gradient.

A concentrating defect can arise from failure of either the gradient or the water-permeability response.

Mineralocorticoid receptors, ENaC and V2 receptors
Correct answer: C.

Spironolactone → intracellular mineralocorticoid receptor.
Amiloride → apical ENaC.
Tolvaptan → basolateral vasopressin V2 receptor.

Aldosterone regulates sodium transport through its receptor; amiloride blocks sodium entry directly.

Learning point: spironolactone and amiloride share potassium-sparing effects through different direct targets.

Diuretic receptor, channel and pump targets
A is wrong: spironolactone blocks the mineralocorticoid receptor; amiloride blocks ENaC.

B is wrong: tolvaptan blocks V2 signalling → ↓ AQP2 insertion; it does not directly plug AQP2.

D is wrong: Na+/K+-ATPase is basolateral and sustains ion gradients; amiloride’s target is apical ENaC.

Learning point: distinguish the drug’s direct target from downstream transport changes.

Potassium-sparing diuresis and aquaresis
Spironolactone and amiloride → ↓ electrogenic distal sodium reabsorption. Less negative lumen → ↓ driving force for potassium secretion. Potassium excretion also depends on distal delivery, flow and potassium availability.

Tolvaptan → ↓ vasopressin-dependent collecting-duct water permeability. Its characteristic effect is aquaresis, rather than the salt-and-water loss of conventional natriuretic diuretics.

Learning point: predict effects from the pathway being interrupted; do not equate every ↑ in urine volume with sodium loss.

Diuretic targets along the nephron
Proximal tubule:
• Mannitol: osmotic water retention in tubular fluid; also acts in other water-permeable segments.
• Acetazolamide: carbonic anhydrase inhibition → ↓ bicarbonate reabsorption.
• SGLT2 inhibitors: ↓ sodium–glucose cotransport → glycosuria and osmotic diuresis, with natriuretic effects.

Thick ascending limb: loop diuretics → NKCC2.
Distal convoluted tubule: thiazides → NCC.
Aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron: spironolactone → mineralocorticoid receptor; amiloride → ENaC.
Collecting duct: tolvaptan → V2 receptor → ↓ AQP2 insertion.

Targets include an enzyme, cotransporters, a channel and receptors. Mannitol acts as an osmole.
