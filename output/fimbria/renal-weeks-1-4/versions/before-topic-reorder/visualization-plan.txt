SHARED VISUAL DESIGN

Use the established dark background (#10151F), Avenir Next typography,
white main text (#EDF3FA), muted anatomical labels (#A4B3C5), and restrained
teal (#65D4C2), gold (#F2C66D) and coral (#EC6174) emphasis.

Place labels beside the structure or pathway they explain. Keep captions
short and use colour consistently within each visual. Omit course branding,
week numbers, learning objectives and answer-choice labels from the visuals.
Question numbers in this planning document are for asset organisation.

Static diagrams: editable vector source plus a 1920 x 1080 PNG. Prepare
layers so they can be revealed in stages during teaching if useful.
Animations: silent captioned 1080p MP4, editable Manim source, representative
frame, comparison image, chapter timings and supporting notes. Existing
animations use Manim Community 0.22.1 at 30 fps.

Q1 — RENAL BLOOD FLOW AND GLOMERULAR PRESSURE

Format: Manim animation, already created; approximately 106 seconds.

Teaching focus: why reducing angiotensin-II-dependent efferent tone can
lower glomerular pressure and GFR when perfusion is compromised.

Sequence: establish afferent inflow, the glomerulus and efferent outflow;
compare angiotensin II, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, prostaglandins, NSAIDs,
SGLT2 inhibitors and calcineurin inhibitors; finish with low perfusion plus
NSAID and ACE inhibitor OR ARB effects.

Keep medicine labels on the affected vessel's side. Preserve the separate,
context-dependent SGLT2 mechanisms and the distinction between an initial
haemodynamic change and long-term kidney benefit.

Q2 — LOOP DIURETICS AND URINE-CONCENTRATING CAPACITY

Format: Manim animation, already created; approximately 61 seconds.

Teaching focus: NKCC2 blockade weakens the medullary osmotic gradient and
reduces maximum concentrating capacity despite high vasopressin.

Sequence: trace tubular flow; show salt leaving the water-impermeable
thick ascending limb; establish water recovery from the collecting duct;
block NKCC2; weaken the gradient and reduce water recovery.

Keep vasopressin high and collecting-duct water pathways available during
blockade. The gradient is reduced rather than abolished.

Existing assets: ../../animations/urine-concentration/
Video: urine-concentration.mp4
Editable source: urine_concentration.py

Q3 — WATER LOAD AND PLASMA SODIUM CONCENTRATION

Format: Manim animation, initial version created; approximately 67 seconds.
Status: return to this later; no further changes have been specified.

Teaching focus: a water load lowers plasma osmolality, suppresses
vasopressin and reduces collecting-duct water permeability, allowing the
excretion of dilute urine and limiting dilution of plasma sodium.

Sequence: establish baseline water recovery; add water while holding the
sodium-symbol count fixed; reduce vasopressin; retrieve AQP2 from the apical
membrane into intracellular vesicles; reduce water reabsorption; increase
urine volume and lower urine osmolality; show movement towards baseline.

Retain the collecting-duct aesthetic of Q2. The principal mechanism here is
reduced permeability. Keep residual water movement and the distinction
between sodium concentration and sodium amount clear.

Existing assets: ../../animations/water-load/
Video: water-load.mp4
Editable source: water_load.py

Q4 — PRERENAL, INTRINSIC AND POSTRENAL AKI

Format: static three-panel anatomical diagram, with optional staged reveal.

Use the same kidney/urinary-tract layout in each panel. Highlight the
different location of the principal problem:
  1. Reduced perfusion after diarrhoea and hypotension: highlight the inlet
     blood supply and show improvement after perfusion is restored.
  2. Acute tubular injury after prolonged shock: enlarge the injured tubular
     epithelium and include a small muddy-brown granular cast inset.
  3. Retention and obstruction: mark the blocked outlet and show bilateral
     upstream collecting-system dilation.

Place the case cue and mechanism beside each panel. Make the comparison
about the mechanism in these three patients, rather than every AKI cause.

Suggested asset folder: ../../illustrations/aki-mechanisms/

Q5 — NEPHRITIC VERSUS NEPHROTIC SYNDROME

Format: layered illustration followed by Manim animation, created;
approximately 87 seconds. This replaces the earlier static-cutaway plan,
following the user's supplied visual reference and request for evolution.

Start with a healthy capillary cross-section showing fenestrated
endothelium, glomerular basement membrane and podocyte foot processes.

Inflammatory pathway: introduce inflammatory cells and swelling; show a
local barrier defect, reduced filtration, and red-cell/protein leakage.
Animate red-cell cast formation in a separate downstream tubular inset.
Associate the state with haematuria, hypertension, reduced GFR and protein
of 1 g/day.

Reset visibly to the healthy barrier before the second pathway.

Podocyte pathway: flatten foot processes and mark slit-barrier dysfunction;
increase albumin leakage and show protein of 5.2 g/day, plasma albumin of
20 g/L, oedema and hyperlipidaemia. Explain that low oncotic pressure and
renal sodium retention both contribute to oedema.

Finish with matched illustrated states and acknowledge possible overlap.
The inflammatory mechanism is illustrative; the sequence does not imply
routine progression from nephritic to nephrotic disease. The new anatomical
illustration uses coral red cells and teal albumin with a visible legend.

Q6 — NEURAL AND MECHANICAL COORDINATION OF MICTURITION

Format: Manim animation, already created; 60 seconds.

Teaching focus: normal voiding combines detrusor contraction with outlet
relaxation through coordinated autonomic and somatic activity.

Sequence: establish storage; introduce filling signals and higher-centre
permission; show pontine coordination increasing pelvic parasympathetic
activity while reducing sympathetic and pudendal activity; relax the outlet
as detrusor contraction begins; show urine flow.

Keep nerve labels beside their pathways, identify S2-S4 for pelvic and
pudendal pathways, and distinguish the bladder outlet from the external
sphincter. Separate the neural-switch phase from active voiding.

Q7 — PARTIAL NEPHRECTOMY AND PRESERVED RENAL TISSUE

Format: static before/after kidney cutaway.

Before: show the 3.2-cm cortical mass confined to the kidney.
After: show removal of the tumour-bearing portion with the remaining
renal tissue preserved. Use a restrained highlight for the excised region
and make the retained kidney visually prominent.

The teaching point is what nephron-sparing surgery achieves in this case.
Keep the question's context visible in a short caption: intervention has
been chosen and partial nephrectomy is technically and oncologically
feasible. No full treatment algorithm is needed.

Suggested asset folder: ../../illustrations/partial-nephrectomy/

Q8 — CLEAR-CELL RCC: BIOLOGY AND ANATOMICAL STAGING

Format: two complementary static panels, with optional sequential reveal.

Molecular panel: VHL loss -> increased HIF signalling -> pro-angiogenic
signalling. Keep the pathway short and connect it to the described tumour.

Anatomical panel: show a renal cortical tumour extending into the renal
vein. Identify renal-vein invasion as T3a, with distant metastasis assessed
separately; this question specifies no distant metastases. Keep local
extension distinct from the M category.

The two panels should teach separate ideas without crowding the molecular
pathway onto the kidney illustration. An authentic histology inset can be
added later if a suitable licensed image is selected.

Suggested asset folder: ../../illustrations/rcc-biology-staging/

Q9 — GLOMERULAR AND NON-GLOMERULAR HAEMATURIA

Format: urinary-tract map paired with urine microscopy comparisons.

Map: distinguish a glomerular origin from downstream urinary-tract sources.
Microscopy: place dysmorphic red cells and a red-cell cast beside the
glomerular example; place predominantly uniform red cells beside the
non-glomerular example. Show clots as a separate macroscopic cue.

Prefer authentic, appropriately sourced microscopy with simplified
annotated drawings alongside. Clearly distinguish schematic drawings from
actual micrographs. Image selection and sourcing remain to be done.

Use 'suggests' or 'supports' rather than treating appearances as proof.
Connect the older smoker's painless visible haematuria with clots to the
need for urological assessment, without assigning an unproven diagnosis.

Suggested asset folder: ../../illustrations/haematuria-sources/

Q10 — THREE MECHANISMS OF PROTEINURIA

Format: Manim animation, already created; approximately 83 seconds.

Teaching focus: distinguish excess glomerular albumin passage, reduced
proximal tubular protein uptake and excess filtered free-light-chain load.

Sequence: establish normal handling; increase albumin passage at the
glomerular barrier; restore barrier restriction and impair tubular uptake;
restore uptake and increase light-chain supply beyond capacity; finish
with a three-column clinical/mechanistic comparison.

Keep uptake active during overflow, residual uptake during tubular injury,
and some albumin uptake during glomerular leakage. The closing caption
acknowledges that mechanisms may coexist.
